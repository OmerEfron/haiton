# Sports-desk craft for Haiton (personal Hebrew newspaper)

Sources: `one.co.il.md` (12 full articles) and `sport5.co.il.md` (11 full articles + 2 short companions). Both sites fetched cleanly; no invented copy.

This summary is for a writer agent that writes **personal life** (work, family, sports-as-fan, food) — not professional match coverage. Steal structure, not soccer jargon.

## one.co.il vs sport5.co.il

**Shared house:** dek is mandatory; `(יום)` is the dateline (not a city slug); color nicknames replace the club name after first mention; length is a type, not a mood; honesty ticks (`על סף`, `טרם הבשיל`, `בטענה ל-`, `מוקדם לדעת`).

**Difference that matters for Haiton:** ONE is a **status desk** (who did what, scoop stamp, deal grammar). Sport5 is a **show desk** (drumroll, colon quotes, labelled explainers, fan "we"). A personal paper needs both: ONE for news/profile discipline, Sport5 for interview/column energy — without copying either site's soccer furniture.

## Map to Haiton product types

### News (ידיעה)
Steal: inverted pyramid + day stamp + dek as a second hed. Sentence one = the fact. Graph 2 = the one number or consequence. Graph 3 = next concrete step. Optional one quote as **proof**, not decoration.

Personal translation:
- Work: `הערב (שלישי) סיימה דנה את הפרויקט שתקע את הצוות שלושה חודשים.` Not a mood weather lede.
- Family: name the event and when (`אמש (שישי)`) before the feeling.
- Fan/food: the result or the dish in sentence one; the scene in graph 2.

Do not write a match report for a Tuesday at the office. No minute-by-minute unless the piece is explicitly a running account of one evening.

### Profile
Steal ONE's Olympic feature: fact on the table first, then reverse into backstory. Childhood/origin graph in the **middle**. Quotes after the picture is set. Closer = an image planted earlier (the smile, the girl in the corner), not a résumé dump.

Personal translation: open on what they are now (the job, the kid, the table they cook at), then the two-years-ago unknown, then the scene that explains the change. Age/role as a fixed epithet once (`דנה, עורכת בת ה-34`), not every sentence.

### Feature
Steal Sport5's labelled slices + ONE's lyric closer. A feature can have subheds (`המצב / לוח הזמנים / ההתאמה`) without becoming an explainer. Mix eye-test and one number. Hedge on the page.

Personal translation: a weekend in the kitchen, a family trip, a season as a fan — thesis in the dek, three labelled beats, end on a visual motif, not "זה היה מדהים".

### Interview
Two house forms, both usable:
1. **Live Q&A** (ONE basketball signing, Sport5 studio): occasion wrap in graph 1, then question as a bare line, answer in quotes. End on the last answer — no reporter moral.
2. **Reported sit-down** (ONE rewrite of a foreign interview): topic blocks, each `סיפר` / `הדגיש` / `הוסיף`, money quote in the lede.

Personal translation: family/work interviews should pick one form and stay in it. Do not mix Q&A with a columnist's "I think". Headline is the sentence they would put on the fridge.

### Column
Steal the split, not the swagger:
- Signed "I" is allowed only when the piece is labelled as voice (`טור`, `פרשנות`).
- Desk news may not say `אנחנו` or issue commands.
- Argument lede: straw-man of the last week's story, then a reversal (`אפשר היה לחשוב ש…` → the title as the slap).
- Close with a demand, a greeting, or next week — not a news logistics line.

Personal translation: the fan-in-the-stands piece, the parent-at-the-table piece, the "I keep ordering the same lunch" piece. First person, parenthetical asides to the reader, one נ.ב. max.

## Craft rules for the personal-life writer

Translate, don't copy.

1. **Dek before body.** Nickname-or-role + the one number that matters + one other name. Columns/interviews: topic list, not a teaser. No naked headline.
2. **Stamp the hour next to the fact.** `הערב (יום)` / `אמש (יום)` / `היום (יום)`. When it happened sits beside what happened. No city dateline needed.
3. **Drumroll is optional and short.** Two words then the fact (`זה קורה:`, `חזר ובגדול.`) — only on news. Columns may open on a question or a false narrative. Features may open on the achievement, then reverse.
4. **One turning point per paragraph.** A dinner, a meeting, a match you watched: one beat per graph (the call, the burnt pan, the 78th-minute equalizer you yelled at). Last graph is the next concrete thing, not a star rating.
5. **Headline is a verdict or a quote, not a label.** `מתנות קטנות:` is the story; the score (or the bill, or the promotion) is the proof. Interviews lead with the fridge-door sentence.
6. **Quote verbs are a ladder.** First `אמר` or `דיבר`. Detail `סיפר`. Volume `הדגיש`. Extra sentence `הוסיף`. Close `סיכם` / `סיכם בזהירות`. Club/family/company voice is `מסרה` or unnamed plural (`אמרו בבית`, `בצוות טוענים`) — never give an unnamed source a name. Prefer colon stacks (`דנה: "…"`) over synonym-dumping `טען`.
7. **Quotes are proof, not furniture.** News: one quote after the fact. Match-like recaps of an evening: almost none — action talks. Interviews: quotes *are* the body, grouped by topic. Features: quotes late, after the scene.
8. **Nickname as grammar, once earned.** After first mention, "הצהובים" / "האדומים" work because the reader already knows the club. In personal copy: "אבא", "המשרד", "השולחן" can replace the full name — but only after you've introduced them. Do not invent color nicknames for a workplace.
9. **Status, shape, then CV — then stop.** Job change, move, new roommate: who + to where + the deal shape (tryout / one year / option). Then a short résumé. Do not invent "this will be great for her career".
10. **Explainers get subheads and "נעשה סדר".** Money, calendar, who is allowed, whether it fits. End on what happens this week.
11. **Analysis asks a question, answers with one number and one role.** `מה מצאה דנה במעבר?` → quiet + two extra hours with the kids. Hedge on the page (`מוקדם לדעת`, `לא מופרך`).
12. **Length is a type.** A yedi'a can be three graphs. A profile runs because it has scenes. Do not inflate a Tuesday lunch into a magazine piece, and do not crush an interview into a scoreline.
13. **Close on a next concrete thing** (news) **or a planted image** (feature) **or the last answer** (interview) **or a demand/greeting** (column). Mix those endings and every piece sounds the same.
14. **Honesty ticks.** Age beside the name once. Not-yet facts stay `על סף` / `טרם הבשיל`. Disputed claims get `בטענה ל-`. Expert voice carries `לעניות דעתי`. Steal the caution.

## Do not (clichés that make every piece identical)

- Weather / "השמש זרחה" / slow scene as a **news** lede.
- Every piece in first person. Desk news has no `אני`; columns do.
- `טען` / `הודה` / `מסר` on a loop. Pick the ladder; don't synonym-spin.
- Headline that is only a topic (`על העבודה`, `סופ״ש משפחתי`) with no verdict and no quote.
- Dek that repeats the headline word-for-word.
- Three synonyms for the same club/person in one paragraph (full name + nickname + place metonym every sentence).
- Quote in sentence one of a news piece *instead of* the fact — unless the product type is interview.
- Moral closer in desk voice (`וזה מלמד אותנו על החיים`). Leave morals to signed columns, and even then prefer a demand or an image.
- Inflating length to sound "writerly". A three-graph yedi'a that stops is more ONE than a 900-word vibe.
- Invented scoop stamps, unnamed sources with names, or "he will be a great signing" forecasts on unfinished facts.
- Match-report furniture in non-match pieces: `בדקה ה-78`, `סגר את הסיפור`, `עשה 0:1` — unless the reader is actually watching a game.
- Fan-column commands (`יאללה, להוציא את הצ'קים`) in a news brief.
- Mixing Q&A, analysis numbers, and lyric childhood in one untitled blob so every Haiton item has the same mushy middle.

## Steal list, one line each (for the writer agent)

1. Dek is a second hed; never skip it.
2. Fact + `(יום)` in the first breath of news.
3. Headline = verdict or fridge-quote, not a folder name.
4. One beat per paragraph; closer matches the type.
5. Quote ladder; unnamed sources stay unnamed.
6. News: quote as proof. Interview: quote as body. Feature: quote after scene. Column: "I" is the warrant.
7. Subheads when explaining; lyric only after the fact is on the table.
8. Hedge unfinished facts; stop when the CV/scene ends.
9. Color/nickname only after first full mention.
10. Signed voice and desk voice do not share pronouns.
11. Endings are typed: next step / last answer / planted image / demand.
12. If two pieces this week share the same lede shape, change the type — not the adjectives.
